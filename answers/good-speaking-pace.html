What Is a Good Speaking Pace?

Updated 2026-10-28

A good speaking pace is about 130 words per minute for conversation and 150 words per minute for an audiobook or a recorded lecture. For a live presentation, 120 words per minute leaves room for pauses and for the audience to follow your slides.

Pace is the number of words you say in one minute. The right pace depends on what the listener must do with your words. A listener who only follows a story can take a faster pace than a listener who must read a number from your slide and write it down.

Two independent data points agree with the middle of this range. Wikipedia's article on speech tempo reports that a typical speaking rate for English is four syllables per second. The same article reports a study of radio bulletins where the average rate ran from 168 words per minute in BBC English to 210 words per minute in Spanish RNE. A presenter who speaks at 120 to 150 words per minute is inside the range that broadcasters use.

Why a live presentation needs a slower pace than a recording

A recording plays to one person at a time, and that person can pause or rewind. A live talk plays once. The listener must also look at your slide, find the number you just said, and return to your voice. A pace of 120 words per minute gives that listener time.

The estimator adds a pause of 0.5 seconds for each sentence on top of the words. That is the smallest realistic pause. A live speaker also stops for a breath, a slide change or a question. Treat the estimator result as the floor of your talk length, not the total.

How to measure your own pace

  1. Read a passage aloud for one minute at the pace you plan to use.
  2. Count the words you read. That number is your personal words per minute.
  3. Paste the same passage into the Speaking Time Estimator and compare the estimate with your count.
  4. If your count is above 150, mark the places where you will pause. A pause lowers the average without changing the words.

Read the same passage three times on three days. Most speakers speed up when the text becomes familiar. The third reading shows the pace an audience will hear.

Signs that your pace is too fast

  • You finish the slide before the audience has read the title.
  • You say "as you can see" and the audience is still looking for the item.
  • You take a breath in the middle of a clause, not at the end of a sentence.
  • Your sentence length falls under 10 words because you are cutting words to keep up.

The Sentence Length Analyzer shows the sentence length pattern of your script. Short sentences at the start of a section usually mark a place where you rushed.
